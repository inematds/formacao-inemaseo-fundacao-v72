Lesson 6 · 18 min

robots.txt by purpose: search, user action, and training

"Block the AIs" has become a common request, and it is almost always worded wrong. Each AI company has different robots for searching, for opening a page at someone's request, and for training models. Blocking one does not block the others. And in 2026 your CDN may be deciding for you.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The three types of AI robot and what happens when you block each one
  2. 2Write the robots.txt in three groups
  3. 3Check your CDN's default blocking
  4. 4Treat llms.txt as a cheap, optional extra

Goal: decide in robots.txt and in the CDN dashboard what to allow for search, for user action, and for training.

01 · Who knocks on the door and what happens if you close it

Tap each block.

Click each block in the diagram to see what it does.

Decide by purpose, write it in robots.txt, and check that your CDN agrees.

02 · Decide with four questions

Click on each one.

1. Do I want to be cited?

For almost every business, yes: allow the search robots. They are how your site gets into the answers.

Blocking search to "protect your content" is usually the worst of both worlds: the text has already been read, and you disappear from the answers.

2. Do I let the AI read on request?

If a customer pastes your link into the assistant and asks for a summary, this is the bot that reads it. Allowing it is usually the natural choice if you sell or teach.

3. Do I allow training?

There's no right answer here. It's a business choice. Blocking training does not take you out of search.

Write down the reason in the robots.txt itself, as a comment, for whoever comes next.

On inema.clubThe robots.txt also allows ClaudeBot, which is a training bot: INEMA is open content and chose to allow it.

4. Does the CDN agree?

Open the CDN dashboard and look for the bot and AI settings. Check whether a default block is turned on, and on which pages.

If the dashboard blocks what the robots.txt allows, the dashboard wins.

03 · The robots.txt in three groups, and the optional llms.txt

Adapt the training decision to yours. Change the sitemap address.

robots.txt · by purpose
# robots.txt by purpose (adapted from the inemaSEO templates/robots.txt)
User-agent: *
Allow: /

# Search: show up and get cited in answers
User-agent: OAI-SearchBot
User-agent: Claude-SearchBot
User-agent: PerplexityBot
User-agent: Googlebot
User-agent: Bingbot
Allow: /

# User action: the AI opens the page at someone's request
User-agent: ChatGPT-User
User-agent: Claude-User
User-agent: Perplexity-User
Allow: /

# Training: your call. This example blocks; blocking training does not take you out of search
User-agent: GPTBot
User-agent: ClaudeBot
User-agent: Google-Extended
Disallow: /

Sitemap: https://www.seusite.com/sitemap.xml
llms.txt · optional (templates/llms.txt, abridged)
# Your Site

> One sentence saying what it is, who it is for, and what it delivers.

## Main pages

- [Home](https://www.seusite.com/)
- [About / who runs it](https://www.seusite.com/sobre/)
- [Catalog](https://www.seusite.com/catalogo/)

## Data for machines

- [Sitemap](https://www.seusite.com/sitemap.xml)
Decision note
Bot decision for <seusite.com> on <mm/dd/yyyy>
- Search: allowed (reason: <...>)
- User action: <allowed/blocked> (reason: <...>)
- Training: <allowed/blocked> (reason: <...>)
- CDN: <name>, default AI block <on/off>, checked in the dashboard
- llms.txt: <yes/no> (optional)
Example from the repository

The method's templates/robots.txt puts ClaudeBot together with the search bots. According to Anthropic's documentation, ClaudeBot collects data for training; search belongs to Claude-SearchBot, and reading on request belongs to Claude-User. The model above separates the three and adds the user action bots.

Ahrefs study (15/06/2026), 137 thousand domains: among valid llms.txt files, 97% received no requests at all. Google's AI guide says you can ignore it.

Honest take: it's cheap and can work as an index for coding agents. It is not a citation lever, and it should not be sold as "get into ChatGPT".

  • Disallow for "all AIs": takes you out of search answers.
  • Thinking robots.txt is a lock: the CDN is what enforces it.
  • Forgetting the CDN: the dashboard may block agents that the robots.txt allows.
