Lesson 3 · 15 min

Search Console and Bing Webmaster: verify your site

The two free dashboards are the only official source for what Google and Bing see on your site: what is indexed, which searches show you, and, since 2026, what appears in the AI features. Before you create a new account, check whether someone has already verified the site. It happens more often than you'd think.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Check in DNS whether the site is already verified
  2. 2Create the domain property in Search Console
  3. 3Connect Bing Webmaster by importing from Search Console
  4. 4Know what the AI dashboards show in 2026

Goal: check whether the site is already verified, then verify it in Search Console and Bing Webmaster, with the sitemap submitted in both.

01 · From DNS to the dashboards

Five steps. Click each one.

Click each block in the diagram to see what it does.

All free. Paid tools don't have data from Google's index: only estimates.

02 · Verify without stumbling

Tap each step.

1. Look for what already exists

Run the dig command on your domain. If google-site-verification shows up, someone has already verified it: find that account and ask for access.

A duplicate account splits the history and confuses whoever looks after the site.

At INEMA (09/23/2026)One plan said "no Search Console" because it only looked in the code. The DNS showed the verification. The rule became: check the TXT record before claiming it doesn't exist.

2. Create the property

In Search Console, choose "Domain" and paste the TXT record it shows into your DNS panel (at the registrar or the CDN).

Propagation can take a few minutes; then click verify.

Why domainOne property covers www, non-www, and subdomains like news. or eventos.

3. Connect Bing

At bing.com/webmasters, sign in with an account and choose to import from Google Search Console.

If you prefer, Bing also accepts its own verification. IndexNow notifies Bing even when the site isn't in this dashboard.

4. Submit and wait

Submit the sitemap in both and note the date. The performance reports start filling up in a few days.

The following week, look at the page list and the sitemap status.

03 · The commands and the record

Replace the domain with yours.

Terminal · is it already verified?
dig TXT seusite.com +short
# look for a line with google-site-verification=
TXT record in DNS (template)
Type:  TXT
Name:  @   (the root domain)
Value: google-site-verification=<code that Search Console shows>
Verification note
Search Console: domain property <seusite.com>, verified on <mm/dd/yyyy>, account <who owns it>
Bing Webmaster: imported from Search Console on <mm/dd/yyyy>
Sitemap submitted: <https://www.seusite.com/sitemap.xml> — status <success / pending>
Simulation: illustrative example
"google-site-verification=AbCdEf123exemplo"
"v=spf1 include:_spf.exemplo.com ~all"

The first line says the site already has an owner in Search Console. The second one is for email and has nothing to do with SEO.

  • Prefix instead of domain: verifies only one version of the address and hides the rest.
  • Deleting the TXT record after verifying: Search Console asks for verification again.
  • Trusting a paid tool to know what's indexed: only the official dashboards have that data.

04 · Practice: both dashboards up and running

Do them in order.

E1 · The dig comes first

10 min

Run dig TXT on your domain and note the result. If there is already a verification, find out whose account it is.

Done when: you can tell whether the site was already verified and who has access.

E2 · Verified and with a sitemap

30 min

Create (or recover) the domain property in Search Console, import it into Bing Webmaster, and submit the sitemap in both.

Done when: both dashboards show the site as verified and the sitemap with a status of success or pending, noted with the date.
