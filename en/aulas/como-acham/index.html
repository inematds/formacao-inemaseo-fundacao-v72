Lesson 1 · 14 min

How Google, Bing, and AI find a website

Writing more pages won't help if the search engine can't read the ones you already have. This lesson shows the path a page takes until it appears in a search or an AI answer, the three principles of the inemaSEO method, and Google's official 2026 yardstick for telling foundation from fad.

02 · The three principles and the 2026 yardstick

Click each tab.

1. Foundation before content

Without the right canonical, and with the sitemap and robots.txt undecided, no new page pays off. That's why this whole course comes before writing.

You start by looking at what already exists, not by creating more.

At INEMAOn 09/23/2026, the /cursos/ page showed up as "Discovered – currently not indexed" and the new pages were unknown to Google.

2. Real data only

Every page rests on a course, project, event, or experiment that actually exists. AI can rewrite and organize; it never makes up facts.

This is what Google itself calls non-commodity content: your own experience, which an AI would not produce on its own.

Quick testIf you swap the topic of the page and the paragraph is still valid, it's generic. Rewrite it with something of your own.

3. Measured without the brand

People who already search for you by name don't prove your SEO works. The number that matters is people who didn't know you and found you.

At the start, almost every click is from the brand, and that is misleading.

INEMA baselineIn the 28 days from 08/25 to 09/21/2026, 639 of 647 clicks were branded.

4. The 2026 yardstick

Google's official guide to AI features (May/2026) says there is no special requirement to appear in AI Overviews and AI Mode: standard SEO best practices apply.

It also says you don't need to create a file for AI or any special schema. Use this yardstick whenever someone sells you a shortcut.

In practiceTechnical foundation + content with your own experience. The rest is a fad until proven otherwise.

03 · The first test: fixed questions on your topic

Copy the spreadsheet and the questions. Replace what's between < > with your own.

Test questions (3 to start)
1. Where can I learn <your topic> in <your city or language>?
2. What is the best way to <problem your business solves>?
3. What is <your business name>?
Spreadsheet (one row per answer)
date;assistant;question;did my site appear? (yes/no);who was cited
<mm/dd/yyyy>;<ChatGPT>;1;no;<cited site>
<mm/dd/yyyy>;<Perplexity>;1;no;<cited site>
Example from the repository

A single round on 07/10/2026, with 20 frozen questions: INEMA appeared in 0 of 48 neutral answers on Claude and in 0 of 4 on Gemini. ChatGPT and Perplexity were not measured.

"What is INEMA?" got 4 of 4 answers about a different agency with the same name: a name collision. Question 3 in the spreadsheet exists to catch this in your case.

  • Answers vary from one day to the next: write down the date and repeat it the same way next month.
  • Zero today is a starting point, not a failure.
  • The full measurement (20 to 30 questions, every month) is in course 4.

05 · What has changed since the video

Three statements from the video that the 2026 yardstick adjusts.

llms.txt. The video calls it a "site map for AIs". An Ahrefs study of 137 thousand domains (06/15/2026) found 97% of the files had no requests at all, and Google says it may ignore it. In this course it is optional and cheap (lesson 6).

robots.txt. The video says to allow the AI bots. That is still right, but in 2026 the decision is by purpose: search, user action, and training are different bots, and blocking training doesn't take you out of search (lesson 6).

40-to-60-word answer. A direct answer at the start helps the reader. No source backs it as a technique for being cited by AI; treat it as clarity.

Two short exercises. Keep your answers: you will come back to them in course 4.

15 min

Done when: each line points to a page on your site or to something that could become a page.
